# Industry application: research report

*Generated: 2026-11-11 | Sources: 11 | Confidence: medium*

Question: does the backstory hold, that effente is for assembly lines and manufacturing equipment that is getting old?

## Summary

Three of the four claims behind the backstory are supported. Downtime costs are confirmed. The skilled-worker shortage is well documented. Smaller manufacturers do lean on fix-it-when-it-breaks maintenance, with cost as a named barrier. The "aging equipment" claim is weaker than it sounds: US factory equipment averages about 9 to 10 years old, not decades. The stronger framing is that most equipment is unmonitored and the people who could judge it by feel are leaving.

## 1. Cost of downtime: confirmed

- Unplanned downtime costs the world's 500 biggest companies about $1.4 trillion a year, roughly 11% of revenue, up from 8% in 2019 ([Siemens, True Cost of Downtime 2024](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:1b43afb5-2d07-47f7-9eb7-893fe7d0bc59/TCOD-2024_original.pdf)).
- A large automotive plant loses $2.3 million an hour, twice the 2019 figure ([Plant Services](https://www.plantservices.com/predictive-maintenance/article/55308021/maintenance-mindset-inflation-driven-trends-are-causing-unplanned-downtime-costs-to-surge-300-in-heavy-industry), [AEMT](https://theaemt.com/resource/the-true-cost-of-downtime-2024-a-comprehensive-analysis.html)).
- The report is based on 181 online interviews at large industrial organisations.

Caveats: the $2.3M figure is for large automotive plants, not all car plants. A "$260,000 per hour average" often credited to Siemens appears to come from Aberdeen Research. Do not use it. These details come from search summaries; open the Siemens PDF before quoting.

## 2. Aging equipment: weaker than the pitch assumes

- Average age of equipment in US durable goods factories was 9.2 years in 2022 and 2023, the highest since 2011 ([Silverado Policy Accelerator](https://silverado.org/publications/assessing-us-competitiveness-durable-goods-manufacturing-investment-trends/), from Federal Reserve and BEA data).
- A trade article puts manufacturing assets at "closing in on 20 years" ([IndustryWeek](https://industryweek.com/leadership/companies-executives/article/22008627/aging-assets-rebuilding-us-manufacturing)). That likely includes buildings, which BEA says run older than equipment ([BEA](https://bea.gov/help/faq/1031)), so it is not comparable.
- A claim that steel plant equipment averages 28 years comes from a vendor blog. Unverified.

Inference: "factories are full of 25-year-old machines" is not supported as a general statement. "Equipment age is at a decade-plus high" is.

## 3. Skilled-worker shortage: supported, numbers vary

- US manufacturing needs about 3.8 million workers by 2033, of which 2.8 million are to replace retirees; up to 1.9 million roles could go unfilled ([IndustryWeek](https://industryweek.com/talent/education-training/article/55323232/manufacturing-day-2025-a-skilled-workforce-should-be-the-nations-first-priority), [Supply Chain 24/7](https://www.supplychain247.com/article/misumi-us-manufacturing-report-workforce-shortage-2026/news), both citing Deloitte and the Manufacturing Institute).
- About 2.3 million technician positions need filling between 2025 and 2030, with roughly half a million open now ([Packaging World](https://www.packworld.com/home/article/22974236/manufacturing-technician-shortage-23m-jobs-by-2030)).

Caveat: all secondary coverage. Figures differ by report and time window. Cite the Deloitte and Manufacturing Institute originals.

## 4. Smaller manufacturers are under-served: supported, not quantified

- Maintenance strategy tracks company size: smaller firms rely more on reactive maintenance with little predictive work ([NIST / PHM Society](https://papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/ijphm/article/download/2883/1748)). The source calls part of this anecdotal.
- About 48% of survey respondents rely heavily on reactive maintenance (same source).
- Cost is a leading barrier to advanced maintenance, with technology support and staffing ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/document/nistdougthomaspdf)).

Gap: no figure found for the share of machines that go unmonitored. Insufficient data.

## 5. Handheld vibration tools already exist

- The vibration analyzer market is about $193 million in 2025, projected to $355 million by 2032, and includes route-based handheld analyzers as a category ([Reanin](https://www.reanin.com/reports/vibration-analyzer-market)). Single source, paid report summary.
- Fluke and SKF sell handheld analyzers. No prices found.

Inference: effente is not competing only with Tractian and Augury. Handheld analyzers from Fluke and SKF are the closer comparison, and judges may know them. The difference to claim is the verified explanation and the unknown alarm: existing handhelds give a trained analyst a spectrum to interpret, while effente gives an untrained technician a diagnosis with a checkable reason. That contrast is an inference and needs a look at one real product before the pitch.

## What this means for the pitch

1. Keep the Siemens figures. Say "large automotive plant".
2. Reframe "old equipment" as "unmonitored equipment and a shrinking pool of experts". It is better supported.
3. Name the buyer: small and mid-size manufacturers who run reactive maintenance because monitoring costs too much.
4. Add Fluke and SKF handhelds to the competitor list.
5. Use the 2.8 million retirements figure for the knowledge-loss point, cited to Deloitte and the Manufacturing Institute.
